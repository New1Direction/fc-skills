# Live collection

Discover installed market-data and chain-analysis tools by capability. For public current facts, use web research and primary sources. Verify provider chain coverage, historical interval semantics, pagination, quote denomination, pool identity, and whether event time differs from availability/publication time before relying on results. Do not assume a provider supplies wallet cohorts merely because it supplies candles. If an applicable provider is unavailable, name the missing capability and continue with retained evidence or a narrower report.

Resolve identifiers from authoritative explorer/issuer/pool sources, not symbols alone. A candidate needs evidence of earlier trading activity followed by adequately observed quiet windows; an old deployment date and flat price do not prove dormancy. Discovery rankings are candidate generators, not findings. Fix window size, historical selection rule, cutoff, and thresholds before evaluating resurgence to reduce selection bias.

Collect historical trades with successful pagination and coverage bounds; normalize swaps to end-user purchasers, exact quote units, chain timestamps, and retained transaction/source identifiers. Preserve failed-page and unsupported-event exclusions. Establish first-purchase history across the token's life or mark it incomplete. Returning holder participation and newly seen wallets are separate from proven first purchases. Group dependency assertions need dated funding/timing evidence and competing explanations; missing relationship data remains unknown.

Capture pool and unit changes, quote reserves, and actual comparable depth at a fixed slippage target. Screen rebases, migrations, new-pool routing, quote-asset changes, wrapped variants, and decimal changes. Do not silently stitch identities. Use a dated incumbent-holder snapshot and swaps against that declared cohort. Keep CEX activity or off-chain campaigns separate from chain observations.

Use only facts whose `available_at` is at or before the explicit cutoff. For historical backtests, present-day data revisions and newly discovered relationships are not known-at-the-time evidence. When historic availability cannot be established, label it unknown and exclude it from a historical decision claim. Retain responses outside the skill directory, compute hashes, and cite direct source records. A normalized file's `observed` label means the submitter declares actual observations; the helper does not authenticate raw chain events.
